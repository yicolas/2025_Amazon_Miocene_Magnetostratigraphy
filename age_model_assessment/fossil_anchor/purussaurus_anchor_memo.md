# Purussaurus as an age anchor for the TAM column

_2026-07-22. Follow-on to the age-model assessment. A Purussaurus (giant caiman) was recovered at Tamshiyacu ~0.5 m above the base of the measured column._

## Short answer

Yes — and it's genuinely useful, but as a **one-sided (older) bound**, not a precise datum. *Purussaurus* is a middle-to-late Miocene genus that does **not exist before ~13.8 Ma**, so its presence means the fossil horizon is **younger than ~13.8 Ma**. The palynology already gave the opposite bound (older than 12.7 Ma). Put together, the two independent anchors **bracket the section to ~12.7–13.8 Ma**, and with the tighter Iquitos-Pebas context (~13 Ma) to **~12.7–13.0 Ma** — which is exactly where the best-fit magnetostratigraphic reversal falls.

## What Purussaurus constrains

| Identification level | Age it implies | Strength as an anchor |
|---|---|---|
| *Purussaurus* sp. (genus only) | ~13.8 → ~6.8 Ma (mid–late Miocene) | Weak on its own, but a firm **ceiling ≤ ~13.8 Ma** |
| → *P. neivensis* (if diagnosable) | ~13.8–11.8 Ma (Laventan, middle Miocene) | Strong — the diagnostically **middle-Miocene** species |
| → *P. mirandai* / *P. brasiliensis* | ~9–5.3 Ma (late Miocene) | Would **break** the middle-Miocene story — watch for this |

The oldest *Purussaurus* anywhere is *P. neivensis*, ~13 Ma, at La Venta (Colombia) **and** in the Pebas Formation near Iquitos. The famous Iquitos Pebas crocodylian assemblage (Salas-Gismondi et al. 2015, *Proc. R. Soc. B*) — which contains *P. neivensis* as its apex predator — is dated to **~13 Ma** by palynology, ostracods and mollusc zonation.

## How it tightens the tie

The single reversal in each site was compatible with **6** mid-Miocene reversed chrons under palynology alone. Adding the fossil ceiling:

| Constraint set | Age bracket | Surviving reversed-chron candidates |
|---|---|---|
| Palyno T15/T16 floor only | ≥ 12.7 Ma | 6 (down to C5ACnr at ~14.1 Ma) |
| + *Purussaurus* genus ceiling | 12.7–13.8 Ma | 5 |
| + Iquitos-Pebas context (~13.0 Ma) | 12.7–13.0 Ma | **3** (C5Ar.1r, C5Ar.2r/.3r region) |

The best-fit **inclination** age models place TAM's reversal at **12.94–13.0 Ma** and SC's at ~13.08–13.14 Ma — both inside the allowed band, and TAM's sits right at the young edge of the tight bracket, i.e. the reversed subchron at the **C5Ar / C5AAn transition (≈ C5Ar.3r / C5AAr, ~12.9–13.0 Ma)**. So the fossil, the palynology, and the magnetostratigraphy now **converge** on the same ~13 Ma tie instead of the analysis floating across 6 candidates. See `fig_purussaurus_bracket.png`.

## Caveats (important before leaning on this)

1. **Species ID does the heavy lifting.** Genus-only gives a 7-Myr window; only *P. neivensis* delivers the tight middle-Miocene age. If the material can be diagnosed to species, the anchor gets much stronger — and a late-Miocene species (*mirandai*/*brasiliensis*) would overturn the ~13 Ma reading, so this is a real test, not just confirmation.
2. **Watch the circularity.** *Purussaurus* in Amazonia is partly *calibrated by* the Pebas palynology, so using "Pebas ~13 Ma" to date a Pebas column is soft. The clean, independent leg is **La Venta**, where *P. neivensis* is dated by mammal biostratigraphy and radiometric ages independent of the Pebas — lean on that for the ceiling.
3. **Stratigraphic placement.** "~0.5 m above where the column started" puts the fossil near the **base** of the section. In the synthesis the basal TAM cores (Group 1: TAM 1/2/3B) are the ones with invalid PCA, so the fossil currently anchors an interval whose *polarity* we haven't recovered. Recovering Group 1's polarity (re-pick over the NRM+10 mT range) would let the fossil pin a polarity zone directly, not just the section as a whole.
4. **No prior Tamshiyacu vertebrate record.** There is no published *Purussaurus* from Tamshiyacu specifically — this would be a new occurrence, which is nice for the paper but means the age transfer rests on the genus/species range, not a pre-dated local horizon.

## What to do with it
- Get the specimen diagnosed to species if at all possible (skull/osteoderm characters) — that's the difference between a 7-Myr and a ~2-Myr bracket.
- Log the fossil's exact height in the column and tie it to the sediment-depth axis used for the polarity synthesis.
- Re-pick TAM Group 1 polarity so the fossil horizon carries a polarity value.
- In the manuscript, present *Purussaurus* as the **independent upper age bound** that complements the palynological lower bound — jointly bracketing to ~12.7–13.8 Ma and supporting the C5Ar/C5AAn (~13 Ma) tie.
